## `Slide 2 — THE ICON`
 
**Headline:** In 1954, they were *just* a pair of jeans.
In 2025, they sold for *$52,000*.

Supporting line: Marilyn Monroe wore these jeans in a 1954 western. They were ordinary shop-bought denim, sewn by someone whose name nobody wrote down. In 1999, they went to auction and in 2025, they sold again for $52,000. All of that value came from the person who wore them and none of it came from the person who made them.

## `Slide 3 — THE STAT`
 
**Headline:** While the jeans got famous, the job of *making* jeans was *disappearing*. 

Supporting line: Over the next decade after the pair first went to auction, the number of people working in US clothing factories fell from about 504,000 to about 161,000. That is roughly 94 jobs gone every day for a decade. The fall had started back in the 1990s and it never really stopped. Today, about 72,000 are left.

## `Slide 4 — THE MECHANISM`
 
**Headline:** Manufacturing jeans *didn't stop*, it *moved*.

Supporting line: About 97 in 100 pieces of clothing sold in the US come from somewhere else. The sewing went to countries where the same work costs for far less. Nobody held a meeting, the industry just did the math and ended up with the same solution.
  
*(footer: 3/7)*

## `Slide 5 — THE PRICE`
 
**Headline:** Meanwhile, the burden got *lighter* at checkout.

Supporting line: By 2010, clothes had gone back down to what they cost in 1990, while everything else cost ~71% more. That gap was real money back in shopper’s pockets and it mattered the most to those with the least to spare. Several things kept prices down, including cheaper sewing and sewing is still done by hand, by someone.
 
Chart 2: U.S. sewing jobs vs. prices
Citation: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2026a, 2026b, 2026c

## `Slide 6 — THE COST`

**Headline:** The burden shifted to the hands behind the seam.

Supporting line: Our clothes are mostly made by workers overseas. In Bangladesh one of the world’s biggest clothing exporters, the legal minimum for a garment worker is ~$101 a month. A US sewing machine operator on typical pay earns that in under six hours. At that minimum, the $52,000 jeans adds up to about 43 years of pay.

Chart 3: Years of pay equal to one $52,000 pair of jeans
Citation: Andaloro, 2025; Paul & Jamkhandikar, 2023; U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2026

*(footer: 5/7)*

## `Slide 7 — THE TAKE`

**Headline:** So, what is a pair of jeans really *worth*?

Supporting line:One pair ended up being worth $52,000 because of who wore it. The value of the work put into making them decreased because of where it moved. In the middle of it all is the price most of us pay, which remained low to help us when money was tight. All three numbers describe the same garment. The saving is real, so are the people covering it.
