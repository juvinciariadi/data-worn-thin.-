# Sources — Issue #4: "All Cut, No Credit."

## Price breakdown (Chart 5.1)

**Data:** Retail price breakdown percentages — Retailer 60%, Brand profit 12%, Materials 12%,
Transport 8%, Factory profit 4%, Worker wages 0.6%

**Primary source:** Clean Clothes Campaign, original breakdown of the retail price of a €29
T-shirt made in Bangladesh, sold in Europe. Originally published as part of CCC's "poverty
wages" campaign materials; the live page has since been reorganized and no longer displays the
original breakdown directly.

**Corroborating sources** (used to verify the primary figures since the original CCC page is no
longer live):
- Statista chart citing Clean Clothes Campaign: https://www.statista.com/chart/34514/breakdown-of-the-retail-price-of-a-t-shirt
- War on Want, "The global impact of the fashion industry," citing Clean Clothes Campaign directly
  with matching euro figures (brand profit and materials each €3.61 / 12%, worker wages €0.18 /
  0.6%): https://waronwant.org/fashioning-future/global-impact-fashion-industry

**Date accessed:** 2026-08-14

**Application note:** The original CCC data point is a specific €29 T-shirt. For this issue, only
the PERCENTAGES were used, applied to a $25 USD hypothetical composite garment (not a currency
conversion of the actual €29 item). This is a deliberate scope choice, not a data substitution.

**Data honesty note:** CCC's own published percentages sum to ~96.6%, not 100%. The ~3.4% gap is
shown on the chart as its own labeled, hatched segment ("unaccounted / rounding in CCC's original
published figures") rather than folded into another category or silently forced to 100%.

## Minimum wage vs. cost of living (Chart 5.2)

**Data:**
- Bangladesh legal minimum wage (RMG sector): BDT 12,500 (~USD 105, as stated directly in the
  source report)
- Cost of living for a Dhaka family: BDT 48,022 (Anker Research Institute, 2025 estimate, as cited
  in the source report)

**Source:** Public Eye & Clean Clothes Campaign, "Squeezed Dry: Pricing pressure in the global
fashion industry," May 2026, p. 24.
URL: https://www.publiceye.ch/fileadmin/doc/Mode/2026_Squeezed_Dry/PE_Report_CCC-Pricing_5-26_E.pdf

**Date accessed:** 2026-08-14

**Conversion note:** The report states the minimum wage figure's USD equivalent directly (BDT
12,500 ≈ USD 105) but does NOT provide a USD figure for the BDT 48,022 cost-of-living number. The
USD value used on the chart ($403.38) is computed by applying the same implied exchange rate from
the report's own minimum-wage conversion (12,500 ÷ 105 = 119.05 BDT/USD) to the cost-of-living
figure, so both numbers sit on a consistent conversion basis. This conversion is mine, not stated
by the source.

**Ratio:** Cost of living is ~3.84x the legal minimum wage ("almost 4x," as stated in the report).

## Context, referenced but not charted

**Data:** Sourcing prices for cotton T-shirts imported into the EU from Bangladesh have declined in
real terms since 2001. Nominal price: +25% (2001–2025). Real price, EU CPI-adjusted: −30%. Real
price, global CPI-adjusted: −50% (roughly halved).

**Source:** Public Eye & Clean Clothes Campaign, "Squeezed Dry," Executive Summary and Figure 7
(p. 13). Same source and URL as above.

**Date accessed:** 2026-08-14

**Note:** These figures are the report's own stated headline comparison (not reconstructed from a
smoothed curve, since the actual year-by-year data includes a sharp 2011 cotton-price spike that a
simple two-point reconstruction would misrepresent). Not plotted as a separate chart in this issue;
referenced only as caption/context to support the "the squeeze has gotten worse, not better, since
the older CCC breakdown was calculated" point in the Findings.
