# Data Worn Thin, Style Guide

## Positioning

Fashion trends, decoded by who actually pays the price: sustainability, labor, and the real cost of "affordable."

**Differentiator:** Plenty of accounts turn data into fashion trend forecasts. This one does that too, but it also asks what a trend costs beyond the hashtag: the labor, the landfill, the wage gap behind the discount. Data with a conscience, not just data with good timing.

**Tone:** Non-judgmental, nuanced. Holds two truths at once, fast fashion is often the rational choice under real financial pressure, and someone else (a worker, the environment, or the consumer's future self via debt) is still absorbing a cost. Never moralizing.

### Design Principles

- Neutral palette carries context. Dusty accent means "here's the issue," a wage gap, a markup.
- Simple bar charts and minimal icon sets, data-forward, never corporate-clinical.
- Consistent typography and grid so posts are recognizable at a glance: serif headline font (Cambria/Georgia), clean sans for labels.
- Sources cited directly on the graphic in small type, non-negotiable for credibility.

### Logo / Brand Mark

Wordmark: "data / worn thin." with a small stitched running-dash motif under "data" (nods to embroidery and the account's thread theme).

DWT monogram, stitched in a running/dash pattern, rendered in Cocoa thread on Cream linen, with an embossed thread look and a subtle woven-linen background texture.

### Footer System (every slide)

- Divider motif: `× > > > > > > > > ×` (short version for tight spaces: `× > > > ×`)
- Page counter, e.g. `01/08`
- Logo lockup, bottom-right
- The `>` chevron doubles as a command-line prompt, the bridge between "stitching" and "coding." The `×` bookends read as a stitch knot, start and finish.

## Slide Labeling Convention

Content slides carry a short noun-phrase tag, small caps, muted, top-left (THE PREMISE, THE CONTEXT, THE APPROACH, and so on). Maintain this convention for every new carousel going forward, for series consistency. Cover and closing slides carry no label.

---

## Post Format (Standard Carousel Structure)

Four-part flow per content-pillar post:

1. **Hook stat**, a single striking number, big and bold.
2. **Break it down**, 2 to 3 slides unpacking the data and its source.
3. **The take**, the creator's opinion, clearly separated from the data.
4. **Caption**, short, cites sources, invites discussion.

---

## Citation Style

APA 7th edition.

- Short in-text citation on the slide, near the stat: *(U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2026)*
- Full reference entry in the post caption, not on the visual:

> U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. (2026). *Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers: Apparel in U.S. City Average* [CPIAPPSL]. FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAPPSL

## AI Use Disclosure

Stated policy, included in the intro post and kept as a permanent bio/highlight item:

> I use AI tools to help draft copy, mock up visuals, and organize research, but every stat is sourced, every citation is checked, and every opinion is mine. Data integrity isn't optional here, and that includes being upfront about the process.

## Bio / Credibility Content

Keep any mention of past internship or project work general, skill and scope only (data cleaning and merging for social impact evaluation). Do not name the specific project or organization.
